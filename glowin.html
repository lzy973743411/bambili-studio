01Context

When expansion becomes a consistency problem

When a platform serves multiple entry points — POS, online booking, and AI phone intake — the real design challenge isn't adding features. It's making sure everything behaves like one system.


As the product expanded, the challenge shifted from building new entry points to keeping behavior consistent across all of them.

02Problem Shift

Three core challenges emerged

01
Intake logic
Different sources had different timing, validation needs, and confidence levels — the same request could behave differently depending on where it came from.
02
State consistency
Once inside operations, requests couldn't be treated as separate flows — inconsistent handling meant staff had to compensate manually.
03
Cross-surface visibility
Merchants needed a clear view of source, status, and next actions — without it, staying on top of activity meant constant context-switching.
03Architectural Strategy

Channel-specific intake.
Consistent downstream behavior.

Three distinct entry points — each with its own rules at intake — converging into one operational model. The system stays flexible where channels differ, predictable where merchants need to act.

POS
Merchant-Created Booking
Created directly by staff. Immediate confidence — auto-confirmed, enters queue instantly.
Online
Online Booking
Submitted by customers. Requires staff review before confirmation enters the queue.
AI Phone
AI Phone Booking
Generated through AI-assisted phone calls. Auto-accepted, AI-tagged for tracking.

Two mechanisms kept the system coherent: source labeling (each request tagged by channel, visible at a glance) and state management (consistent status behavior across all entry points).

06Design Decisions

The real work was in
rules, states, and edge cases

Decision 01
Channel-specific intake, unified downstream
Rather than normalizing all channels at intake, I preserved channel identity through source labeling — so different rules could apply without fragmenting the operational model.
Decision 02
Source labeling as ambient context
Each booking tagged by channel, visible at a glance without requiring staff to investigate. This wasn't a feature — it was a prerequisite for reducing the manual compensation that was already happening.
Decision 03
Explicit state model over implicit behavior
Merchants could trust the behavior without needing to understand the logic. New features evaluated against an existing rule model — which shifted how the team collaborated internally.
07Impact & Outcomes

The UI made the logic self-evident

Sales demos became significantly faster. Onboarding required less explanation — merchants could identify source and next actions immediately.

08Reflection

I initially approached this as an interface problem, but the real work turned out to be in rules, states, and edge cases. Working in a startup where both the PM and I were doing this for the first time, we relied heavily on early customers — a small group, but loyal ones whose feedback shaped a lot of the decisions. We figured things out as we went, which meant the system had to be flexible enough to absorb changes without breaking existing behavior.
