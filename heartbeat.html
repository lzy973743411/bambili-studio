01 · About the Project

Music as identity

A social app for finding people nearby through the music they listen to. Taste comes first; photos come second.

Picture a quiet Tuesday night. Someone rides the train home, one earbud in, the same song on repeat, and nobody around them knows.

Posting it feels like exposing a diary. Meeting people feels like handing over a photo. Heartbeat starts from that moment: what if the stranger two seats away, hearing the same song, could simply say so?

02 · Problems

Photo-first isn't for everyone

The current social landscape is built around appearance. That excludes a lot of people.

1

Mainstream social apps tend to lead with photos, which makes identity visual by default.

2

For people who aren't confident about how they look, a photo-first app can feel uncomfortable, and starting a conversation is harder.

3

People who would rather not share photos have few ways to take part on photo-first platforms.

03 · Research · User Interview

Listening to listeners

Six questions that shaped the design direction. What we heard comes first; open any card for the question and the full answer.

  1. Insight 1

    Listening is a private, daily ritual.

    Question and answer

    Q1. How often do you find yourself listening to music alone?

    A1. Most interviewees listen alone every day. Music is a private ritual, rarely shared in real time.

  2. Insight 2

    Genre is identity, not just preference.

    Question and answer

    Q2. What genres or types of music do you enjoy most?

    A2. Tastes varied widely, but people felt strongly about them. Genre reads as identity, not just preference.

  3. Insight 3

    Taste feels too personal to post.

    Question and answer

    Q3. Have you ever hesitated to share musical interests on social media?

    A3. Yes. Fear of judgment was common: many don't share music publicly because taste feels too personal to expose.

  4. Insight 4

    A shared song is an easier opener.

    Question and answer

    Q4. Are you comfortable initiating conversations with new people?

    A4. Most find it hard to start conversations cold. A shared song feels like a much lower-stakes opener.

  5. Insight 5

    People want this, if it isn't creepy.

    Question and answer

    Q5. Have you ever wished you could discover people who share your musical tastes?

    A5. Almost everyone said yes. They had imagined it, but assumed it wasn't possible without feeling creepy.

  6. Insight 6

    Interested, if it feels safe and music-first.

    Question and answer

    Q6. Are you interested in a social app connecting you with nearby music enthusiasts?

    A6. High interest, with one condition: it must feel safe and music-first, not like another dating app.

A song as the opening line

Photo-optional by design

Photos are optional, not banned. Illustrated avatars let anyone join without one, and when photos are used, they come after taste, not before.

05 · One decision, end to end

Let the song go first

Photos aren't banned in Heartbeat. They come second. One decision, traced from what I heard, to what I chose, to what changed on screen.

Observation

Taste feels too personal to post, and cold openers are hard.

Insights 3 and 4. People wanted to be found through music, but not to perform it, and most found it hard to start a conversation with nothing to say.

Choice

Taste narrows the field. Photos help you choose.

Matching first finds people nearby who listen to music like yours. Only then do photos come in, to help you pick among people you already share something with.

What changed on screen
Iteration · 2026

Lead with why you matched.

In the 2022 card, the photo took most of the space and the taste signal sat in a small row underneath. The iteration puts what you share on top, gives the photo less room, and makes the shared song the way in: the main action sends it, and the conversation opens on that track.

06 · High Fidelity Design

The rest of the app

One possible journey through the app, tab by tab. It isn't a fixed order: Music, Explore, Matching, Chat and Profile are all open from the bottom bar.

  1. Music

    The app opens as a music player. Its home screen includes Nearby are listening, where people around you appear as the songs they are playing.

  2. Matching settings

    Matching can be narrowed by distance, gender, MBTI and tags. The matching screen and the match card themselves are shown in 05.

  3. Chat

    Matches and conversations live in the Chat tab, and a track can be attached to any message. The full conversation is shown in 05.

  4. Explore and Profile

    Explore is its own tab: a feed of posts from people you follow, where a post can start from a song. Profiles show listening stats, playlists and photos.

Screens shown in 05, plus one more state

Deep plum and rose throughout, to keep the night-time, one-earbud-in mood of listening alone.

The idea, in the wild
