01 — About the Project

Music as identity

A social app for finding people nearby through the music they listen to.

Picture a quiet Tuesday night. Someone rides the train home, one earbud in, the same song on repeat — and nobody around them knows.

Posting it feels like exposing a diary. Meeting people feels like handing over a photo. Heartbeat starts from that moment: what if the stranger two seats away, hearing the same song, could simply say so?

02 — Problems

Photo-first isn't for everyone

The current social landscape is built around appearance. That excludes a lot of people.

1

Social media primarily revolves around photo-sharing, making identity visual by default.

2

People who lack confidence in their appearance feel excluded or uncomfortable — and find it hard to start a conversation.

3

Users who prefer not to share photos find mainstream platforms less safe, and concerns about photo theft and identity go unaddressed.

04 — Solutions

Connect through sound

Three design decisions, one for each problem.

♪
Answers problem 1

Music as first impression

Matches and profiles are built from what you listen to — genre, now playing, playlist — so taste, not looks, is the first thing people see.

✦
Answers problem 2

A song as the opening line

A shared track gives you something to say, so starting a conversation no longer depends on confidence or appearance.

◇
Answers problem 3

Photo-optional by design

Illustrated avatars replace photos. Nothing personal to post, and nothing to steal or misuse.

12 — Afterword

The idea, in the wild

Heartbeat was a solo concept project from 2022 — research, interviews, UX and UI, all by me — and it was never built. In the past two years I've come across real products working on the same problem.

Echoes decision 01 · Music as first impression

Turn Up

Shows people nearby who love the same artists, songs and music styles, with shared artists listed first on every profile.

turn-up.app → Echoes decision 02 · A song as the opening line

Meet Music

Connects you with people listening to the same song right now. No swiping, no bios, and an anonymous chat until you choose to reveal yourself.

meetthemusicapp.com → Echoes decisions 01 and 02

MusicMate

Scores how well your music matches someone else's, shows the songs you both saved, and suggests conversation starters from them.

App Store →

None of this proves my design would have worked. It does suggest the problem is real: several teams reached for the same answer — taste before looks — independently of me.
